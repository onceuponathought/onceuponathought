An Open Canvas

I realised something yesterday. One of those quiet realisations that sneaks up on you and then suddenly makes a lot of sense.

We don't just dream. We design.

When I think about having a home of my own, I don't simply wish for a home. In my head, I've already chosen the room layouts, settled the aesthetic, imagined the light coming through the windows at a particular time of day. We do the same with careers, relationships, the shape of our daily lives. We don't carry an idea. We carry an entire picture, fully formed, almost architectural.

And I think that's where a lot of our anxiety quietly lives.

When life doesn't seem to be moving towards that exact image, when the timeline shifts or the path looks different from what we'd drawn up, it starts to feel like something is going wrong. Like we're falling behind. Like we've lost the thread.

But what if the problem isn't the dream? What if it's the amount of detail we've already decided it must have?

There might be a gentler way to want things. To carry the idea of a home rather than the fully finished version of one. Because here's the thing — by the time the right moment arrives, you'll be a slightly different person than you are today. That person deserves to have a say in how the dream takes shape too.

There's a version of dreaming that leaves room for life to happen. Where you hold your desires loosely enough that they can shift and breathe and grow as you do.

An open canvas, not a finished painting.

— Kangkana Devi
